Carports, Sheds & Portals

A portal frame is a rigid frame of columns and rafters that gives a building a clear span with no internal posts, which is why carports, garages, workshops, farm sheds and warehouses use them. We design steel and timber portal frames and light structures across Auckland and New Zealand, with the drawings, calculations and PS1 council needs.

GV Reviewed by Gajanthan Vethanathan, CPEng #1030007 · Director, Stable Structure Updated 24 November 2026

Do carports and sheds need consent?

Schedule 1 of the Building Act exempts some small structures from building consent:

  • Carports at ground level up to 20 m², or up to 40 m² where a licensed building practitioner designs or builds it
  • Single-storey detached buildings, such as sheds, up to 10 m², with no sanitary facilities or potable water storage
  • Detached buildings from 10 to 30 m², when built from lightweight materials to Acceptable Solution B1/AS1 or designed or built by a licensed building practitioner, with conditions on distance from boundaries and other buildings
  • Pergolas, of any size

Exempt structures must still meet the Building Code, and planning rules may still apply. Anything bigger, closer to a boundary, or with long spans, steel portals or heavy use needs consent and specific engineering. The Building Act on legislation.govt.nz sets out every condition.

How a portal frame works

Instead of relying on internal walls, a portal frame resists wind and earthquake through stiff connections at the knees and ridge, and through its base connections into the foundations. That makes the connections and footings the critical parts of the design. We design the frame members, haunches, base plates, purlins and girts, the bracing in the roof and walls, and footings that resist uplift and overturning, with loads to AS/NZS 1170, steel to NZS 3404 and timber to NZS 3603.

Wind is usually the governing load

Light buildings are more likely to be lifted or pushed over by wind than damaged by an earthquake. Large roller doors matter too: an open door lets wind pressurise the inside of the building, which adds to the uplift on the roof. We design for the doors you will actually have, and for the wind exposure of your site.

Carports attached to a house

An attached carport loads the house it is fixed to, and the connection has to carry uplift as well as weight without causing leaks. We check the existing structure, design the fixing, and keep the roof and gutter details weathertight.

Rural and large sheds

Farm sheds, implement sheds and workshops often need wide clear spans, mezzanines or large doors. We design efficient frames that use standard steel sections where possible, so your fabricator can price and build them without surprises.

Timing and cost

A carport or standard shed design typically takes one to two weeks from receiving your layout. Larger industrial buildings take longer and are scheduled with your builder or fabricator. We quote a fixed fee in writing before we start.

What we need to start

The building’s size and layout, door sizes and positions, the site address, and how the building will be used: a carport, storage, a workshop or rural use. If you have a kitset supplier or fabricator lined up, send their drawings too. From that we confirm whether consent is needed and quote a fixed fee, usually within one business day.

Common questions

Can I use a kitset shed?
Many kitset sheds come with engineering for set wind conditions. If your site is more exposed, the shed is altered, or council asks for site-specific design, we can review or redesign the frame and footings.
Steel or timber portals?
Steel gives longer spans in slimmer members; timber suits smaller spans and a warmer look. We design both and recommend what suits your span and budget.
Do you design the foundations too?
Yes. Portal footings carry uplift and overturning as well as weight, so we design them with the frame.

Clear spans that stay standing

Clear spans, honest steel and documentation council will accept the first time: that is what a good portal frame looks like on paper and on site.
