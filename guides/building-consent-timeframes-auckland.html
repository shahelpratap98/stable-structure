How long does building consent take in Auckland?

The 20 working day clock, what pauses it, the 12-month lapse rule and code compliance, explained by a chartered engineer.

Request a Free Quote WhatsApp us
Guide

Under the Building Act, council has 20 working days to grant or refuse a building consent once it receives the application. That clock pauses whenever council asks for more information, so the real time from lodgement to consent depends mostly on how complete and consistent your documentation is.

GV Reviewed by Gajanthan Vethanathan, CPEng #1030007 · Director, Stable Structure Updated 24 November 2026

The 20 working day clock

Section 48 of the Building Act gives a building consent authority 20 working days after receiving an application to grant or refuse it. Working days exclude weekends and public holidays, and the Act also excludes every day from 20 December to 10 January, so an application lodged in early December takes longer than the calendar suggests.

What pauses it: requests for information

If council needs more information, it can ask within those 20 days, and the clock is suspended until it receives the answer. Every request for information (RFI) adds the time it takes to respond plus the time council takes to review the response, so a single RFI can add weeks.

Common reasons for engineering RFIs

  • Drawings and calculations that disagree, such as a beam on the plan that never appears in the calculations
  • A bracing design that does not match the floor plan
  • Missing geotechnical information for foundations or retaining walls
  • Details copied from another job that do not fit this one
  • A producer statement that is missing or does not cover all the engineered work

How to keep the clock moving

Bring the engineer in while the drawings are being finished, not after. Have the drawings and calculations produced together so they agree. Include the geotechnical report if there is one, and make sure the PS1 covers everything that was specifically engineered. Our building consent documentation service is built around exactly that, and we answer council’s engineering questions directly.

After consent: the 12-month rule

A building consent lapses if the work has not started within 12 months of the consent being issued, unless council allows a longer period. If your build might be delayed, talk to council before the year is up.

Inspections and code compliance

During construction, council inspects at the stages listed in your consent, and engineers carry out any construction monitoring it requires. When the work is finished you apply for a code compliance certificate, and council has 20 working days to decide whether to issue it, again paused by any request for information. If no one applies within two years of the consent being granted, council must make that decision anyway, so it pays to apply on time with your PS4 and other documents ready.

Common questions

Can I start work while my consent is being processed?
No. Building work that needs a consent cannot start until the consent has been issued.
Does a PIM speed up my consent?
A project information memorandum tells you about the site and other requirements before you design, which can prevent surprises, but it does not shorten council’s 20 working days.
What if council asks about the engineering?
We respond directly to council on structural and civil questions, whether or not we produced the original design.

Sources

Processing times are in sections 48 (building consent) and 93 (code compliance certificate) of the Building Act 2004, consent lapsing is section 52, and working days are defined in section 7. This guide is general information, not advice for a specific site.
